Can You Wear a Continuous Glucose Monitor or Insulin Pump During an MRI?

November 17, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Usually not. Per a 2021 review in Current Diabetes Reports, insulin pumps and most glucose sensors come off before an MRI. The main exceptions are Abbott's FreeStyle Libre 2, 2 Plus, 3 and 3 Plus sensors, which can stay on under set scan limits, and Eversense's implanted sensor, whose transmitter still comes off.

Why do diabetes devices matter in the MRI room?

An MRI scanner uses a strong magnet that is always on, plus radio waves that can heat metal. The FDA warns that the MR environment can pull on devices, cause heating, and make electronic medical devices malfunction. A device can also blur the images near it.

The FDA sorts devices into three labels:

  • MR Safe: no metal, does not conduct electricity, and has no known hazards in any MRI.
  • MR Conditional: safe only when the scan matches the conditions in the device labeling.
  • MR Unsafe: must not enter the scanner room. The FDA says a device with an unknown status should be treated as MR Unsafe.

Glucose sensors and insulin pumps contain batteries, antennas, and small metal parts. So the answer depends on the exact device you wear, not on diabetes devices as a group.

Which glucose monitors can stay on during an MRI?

Labeling differs by brand and even by model. Here is what each maker says on its own support pages:

  • FreeStyle Libre 2, 2 Plus, 3 and 3 Plus: Abbott's US guidance says these sensors do not need to come off for X-ray, CT, or MRI. On a 3 Tesla scanner, scans between the pelvis and the breastbone are limited to 12 minutes, with a 2-minute cooling break between scans. On a 1.5 Tesla scanner, scanning can run up to 1 hour.
  • Dexcom: Dexcom's FAQ says not to wear any Dexcom CGM system during MRI, because the magnetic field may damage parts and cause wrong readings or missed alerts. The G6 FAQ says to stop the sensor session and remove the sensor first.
  • Medtronic Guardian: the MiniMed 780G FAQ recommends removing the sensor and transmitter for X-ray, CT, or MRI to avoid damage, then inserting a new sensor afterward.
  • Eversense (implanted under the skin): the NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde MRI physics service lists the implanted sensor as MR Conditional, but the smart transmitter on top is MR Unsafe and must be removed.

Two cautions apply. First, the Libre change is US labeling. The same NHS page notes that UK Libre sensors are not yet MR Conditional under the UK standard. Second, older models can carry different rules than newer ones. Check the user guide for the exact sensor you wear.

Do insulin pumps have to come off for an MRI?

Yes. None of the maker or hospital guidance reviewed for this article allows a pump inside the scanner. The 2021 Current Diabetes Reports review advises removing the pump and the infusion set for MRI. You then need a new infusion set ready to restart the pump.

Patch pumps follow the same rule. Insulet's safety page says the Omnipod 5 Pod, sensor, and transmitter must be removed before MRI, CT, or diathermy, and the Controller and smartphone should stay outside the procedure room. For Omnipod DASH, it says to remove and dispose of the Pod before an X-ray, MRI, or CT scan and keep the PDM outside the treatment area.

Treat every handheld part the same way. Insulet's advice to keep the Controller and phone outside the room is a good rule for any CGM receiver, pump controller, or phone.

What happens if a sensor stays on during the scan?

For devices labeled MR Unsafe, the maker has not shown that they are safe in the magnet. The main concerns are damage to the electronics, false readings or missed alerts afterward, heating, and image blur near the device. That is why staff will ask you to remove them, even for a scan of a distant body part.

For the Libre sensors that can stay on, Abbott says readings may be off during the MRI and return fully to normal after 1 hour. In bench testing by Abbott scientists published in 2023, the magnetic pull on Libre sensors in a 3 Tesla scanner was 0.063 to 0.132 newtons. That is more than 100 times smaller than the 15.97 newtons needed to pull a sensor off the body. The NHS physics page adds that a Libre sensor can cause an image artifact of up to 5.8 or 6.9 cm, depending on the model. If the area of interest is right under your sensor, the radiologist may still ask you to remove it.

How is blood sugar managed while devices are off?

Taking off a pump stops insulin delivery. Taking off a sensor stops readings and alarms. Both gaps matter, and both are common. In a 2023 Washington University survey of 99 CGM users, imaging was the most frequent medical reason for losing CGM use, reported by 41.7% of people. The authors linked these gaps to high and low blood sugar events and to the lack of spare sensors.

The 2021 hospital review advises that if a pump will be off for more than 2 hours for imaging, the patient should switch to injected insulin for that time. Your diabetes care team decides how. Follow your diabetes care team's plan for insulin and glucose checks around the scan. Do not invent your own adjustment on scan day.

If you feel shaky, sweaty, or confused during the scan, press the call button. If you are unsure afterward whether symptoms point to a low or something else, symptom.md can help you sort them out before you call your care team.

How should you plan an MRI if you wear a CGM or pump?

A little planning turns a lost sensor and a high glucose day into a smooth visit. Use this checklist, built from the maker guidance above:

  1. Tell the scheduler the brand and model of your sensor and pump when you book. Ask whether the center lets your device stay on, and under what limits.
  2. Ask your diabetes care team for a plan covering insulin and glucose checks while devices are off, especially if the pump will be off for more than 2 hours.
  3. Time the sensor change. Dexcom suggests scheduling the scan near the end of a sensor session so you do not waste a sensor.
  4. Bring a spare sensor and a spare infusion set or Pod, plus your meter, test strips, and fast-acting sugar.
  5. Leave the controller, receiver, and phone in the locker or with a companion outside the scanner room.
  6. Check readings after the scan. If you kept a Libre sensor on, Abbott says readings return fully to normal after 1 hour. Confirm with a fingerstick if a reading does not match how you feel.

The same screening applies to other devices. Read our guides on pacemakers and defibrillators and on what to expect at your first MRI.

The bottom line

Plan to remove your insulin pump and, in most cases, your glucose sensor before an MRI. FreeStyle Libre 2, 2 Plus, 3 and 3 Plus sensors can stay on in the US under Abbott's scan limits. Eversense's implanted sensor stays, but its transmitter comes off. Tell the imaging center your exact device, bring spares, and follow your diabetes care team's plan for the time your devices are off.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I keep my CGM on for a CT scan or X-ray instead of an MRI?

It depends on the brand. Dexcom says G7 and ONE+ users may keep the sensor on for X-ray or CT if it stays out of the scanned area and is covered with a lead apron, after talking with their provider. Medtronic recommends removing the Guardian sensor and transmitter, and Omnipod says to remove the Pod before CT.

Will my insurance or the maker replace a sensor I removed for an MRI?

Policies vary by maker and by plan, so call the manufacturer's support line before your scan. A 2023 survey found that a lack of spare sensors was a common cause of gaps in CGM use. Asking ahead lets you bring a spare.

Does the MRI affect my insulin if the pump is outside the room?

No. A pump left outside the scanner room is not exposed to the magnet. The issue is that you get no insulin while it is off, so you need a plan from your diabetes care team for that time.

Is the Eversense sensor removed for an MRI?

No. The implanted Eversense sensor is MR Conditional and can stay under the skin within set scanner limits. The smart transmitter worn on top is MR Unsafe, so you take it off before the scan and put it back on afterward.

What if the MRI technologist gives different advice from the device maker?

The imaging center's MR safety staff make the final call for their scanner. Some centers use stricter rules than the labeling allows. Bring your device's user guide or the maker's MRI page so staff can review it.
