What Is the Difference Between a 1.5T and a 3T MRI?

October 12, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: The number is the strength of the magnet, measured in tesla (T). A 3T scanner has a magnet twice as strong as a 1.5T scanner. That gives more signal, which the radiologist can use for finer detail or a shorter scan. It does not always give a better diagnosis. In several head-to-head reviews, 1.5T and 3T had similar accuracy, and 1.5T can be the better choice near metal or with some implants.

What do 1.5T and 3T mean on an MRI?

The "T" stands for tesla, the unit for the strength of the scanner's main magnet. The magnet is always on. It lines up protons in your body so that radio waves can make them give off a signal.

A stronger magnet gives a stronger signal. A review in Radiographics states that the primary advantage of 3T over 1.5T is a higher signal-to-noise ratio. The team can use that extra signal as it is, or trade it for sharper detail or a shorter scan. The same review adds that the real gain is limited by other factors.

Both strengths are standard clinical equipment. The FDA treats a research MRI as a significant risk device only when the main magnetic field is greater than 8 tesla for adults, children, and infants older than 1 month. A 1.5T scanner and a 3T scanner are both far below that line.

Some scanners run at lower strengths. Our guide to open MRI versus closed MRI accuracy covers those machines.

What does a 3T scanner do better?

A review in Radiology lists the possible advantages of 3T. They include a higher signal-to-noise ratio and better image contrast, especially on scans that use gadolinium contrast. In practice, the authors write, these can mean images with higher spatial resolution and faster imaging.

The gain is larger for some exams than for others. The Radiographics review found these differences in heart and vessel imaging:

  • More effective at 3T: MR angiography (pictures of blood vessels), first-pass perfusion scans, myocardial tagging, and MR spectroscopy.
  • Comparable at 1.5T and 3T: delayed-enhancement scans, flow measurement, and black-blood sequences.

The same review notes that 3T is well established for brain and for muscle and joint imaging.

The brain gives one clear example. A study in European Radiology scanned 40 patients with a first episode that suggested multiple sclerosis. Each patient had a scan at 1.5T and a scan at 3T. The 3T scans showed 13% more white matter lesions and 7.5% more contrast-enhancing lesions.

Does 3T give a more accurate diagnosis than 1.5T?

Not always. A sharper picture does not always change the answer. Two pooled analyses show this.

Prostate cancer staging. A 2022 meta-analysis pooled four studies in which the study population had scans at both strengths. Pooled sensitivity was 69.5% at 3T and 70.6% at 1.5T. The difference was not statistically significant. The authors called for larger trials that compare the two directly.

Wrist ligament tears. A 2024 meta-analysis in Arthroscopy pooled 37 studies with 3,893 ligament lesions, each checked against wrist arthroscopy. Sensitivity was 0.78 at 1.5T and 0.73 at 3T. Specificity was 0.81 at 1.5T and 0.90 at 3T. The authors found no significant difference between the two.

These results do not prove that the two strengths are equal for every exam. The prostate analysis had only four studies. The wrist studies used different coils and scan settings. The results do show that field strength is one factor among several. The coil, the scan protocol, how still you stay, and the radiologist who reads the images all matter.

The scan is also only one step. If you are still working out what a symptom means, symptom.md explains common symptoms in plain language.

When is 1.5T the better choice?

A stronger magnet also makes some problems larger. The Radiology review names three challenges of body imaging at 3T: increased susceptibility effects, a less even radiofrequency field, and a higher specific absorption rate (SAR). SAR is the rate at which the body absorbs radio wave energy.

Susceptibility is the important one near metal. Metal distorts the magnetic field around it. RadiologyInfo.org, from the American College of Radiology and the Radiological Society of North America, explains that a metallic implant may cause signal loss or alter the MRI pictures. That distortion grows with field strength. A 2025 study of dental implants measured the field distortion around titanium and zirconia implants at 0.55T, 1.5T, and 3.0T. The extent and the amplitude of the distortion increased at each step.

For this reason, a radiologist may choose 1.5T when the area of interest is close to metal. Special scan techniques also reduce the distortion. Our guide to MRI with a hip or knee replacement covers them.

A 1.5T scan is not a lower-quality scan. A 2022 study of multiple sclerosis notes that 1.5T scanners are prevalent in clinical settings.

Does field strength matter if you have an implant?

Yes. The FDA states that patients with an implanted device should not receive an MRI exam unless the device has been positively identified as MR Safe or MR Conditional. An MR Conditional device is safe only in an MR environment that matches its conditions of safe use. Field strength is one of those conditions.

You cannot assume that a label for one strength covers the other. FDA guidance on device labeling states that a device that is MR Conditional in a 1.5T system may be unsafe in higher or lower field systems. The guidance explains that device heating may be greater or less at a different field strength. The result depends on the size and shape of the device.

Heating does not always rise with the field. A 2021 phantom study in Investigative Radiology tested four total hip replacement constructs in gel. The maximum temperature rise at any single point was 13.1°C at 1.5T and 1.9°C at 3.0T. The gel had no blood flow. When the authors simulated blood flow, the heating at 1.5T dropped to the upper physiologic range.

Give the imaging center the implant card or the exact make and model before your appointment. The MRI team checks the label against the scanner.

Is a 3T MRI louder or less safe?

Noise. One Japanese study measured the noise of a 3T scanner. The sound level was higher than at 1.5T in all sequences. Peak sound pressure at 3T ranged from 125.7 dB to 130.7 dB. The study is from 2007 and used one scanner, so your scanner can differ. The authors proposed wearing both earplugs and headphones during 3T exams. Our article on why an MRI machine is so loud explains the cause of the noise.

Heat. The FDA states that the radiofrequency energy used during an MRI could lead to heating of the body. The Radiographics review states that the higher radio frequency at 3T results in a higher SAR. Scanners monitor SAR at every field strength. In the FDA guidance on research scanners, the significant risk threshold is a whole-body SAR above 4 W/kg averaged over 15 minutes. The guidance does not give a different number for 3T.

Overall risk. Neither strength uses ionizing radiation. The FDA calls adverse events from MRI scans very rare.

The bottom line

A 3T MRI has a magnet twice as strong as a 1.5T MRI. The extra signal can give finer detail or a shorter scan, and it helps most in exams such as MR angiography and some brain imaging. It does not make every diagnosis more accurate: pooled studies of prostate staging and wrist ligament tears found no significant difference. A 1.5T scanner can be the better choice near metal, and some implants are labeled for one field strength only. Ask your doctor or the imaging center which strength fits your exam, and report every implant before the scan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 3T MRI always better than a 1.5T MRI?

No. A 3T scanner gives more signal, but pooled studies of prostate cancer staging and wrist ligament tears found no significant difference in accuracy between 1.5T and 3T. The better choice depends on the body part, the clinical question, and any metal in your body.

Does a 3T MRI take less time than a 1.5T MRI?

It can. Reviews in Radiology and Radiographics state that the extra signal at 3T can be traded for faster imaging or for higher resolution. The imaging team decides how to use it, so a 3T exam is not always shorter.

Can I have a 3T MRI if my implant is labeled for 1.5T?

Do not assume so. FDA guidance states that a device that is MR Conditional in a 1.5T system may be unsafe in higher or lower field systems. The MRI team must check the label for your exact device against the scanner.

Does a 3T MRI use radiation?

No. The FDA states that MR images are made without ionizing radiation at any field strength. Both 1.5T and 3T scanners use a magnetic field and radio waves.

How do I find out the strength of the scanner at my imaging center?

Ask when you book. The imaging center can tell you the tesla number of the scanner planned for your exam. If your doctor wants a specific strength, ask that the order states it.
